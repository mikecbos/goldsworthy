The framework comparison
1
The methodological move
Modern critical scholarship separates Pauline and Johannine traditions to preserve exegetical autonomy. The Prologue is read as a self-contained theological statement within the Johannine corpus — its relationship to Pauline logos usage is bracketed.
2
What's suppressed
The shared indwelling motif across both corpora. Paul's Christos en humin and John's pneuma tēs alētheias use identical locative constructions with second-person plural. Five peer-reviewed sources affirm the grammatical parallel. The concept divergence above shows EHC scholars engage five dimensions developmental scholars do not.
3
The patristic accounting
The patristic reading of these texts as a unified witness was not harmonistic overreach — it was attending to the grammatical structure the texts themselves present. Justin, Irenaeus, and Origen all read John's logos in continuity with the Pauline eikōn tradition.
4
The presupposition
"The Bible cannot possess a unified supernatural theology." The developmental camp's consistent narrowing on this text — from six conceptual dimensions to one — is not a finding. It is the consequence of a presupposition that predetermines the result.
5
The disguise
Shrouded in the language of "historical context" and "tradition-history." But Dunn's own citation pattern bridges Paul and John across ten shared concepts — including on this very text. The methodology claims separation; the scholar's own work demonstrates convergence.

If both camps agree on the grammatical distinction in John 1:1 — theos without the article for the Word, ho theos for the Father — and if developmental scholars' own citation patterns bridge the corpora their methodology insists on separating — the question the data surfaces is not "who is right?" but "whose starting point are you inheriting, and have you examined why?"
